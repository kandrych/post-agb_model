What this model does—and what it assumes

A literature-informed, rotatable 3D schematic of a disk-bearing post-AGB binary. It is not a hydrodynamic solution, a radiative-transfer model, or a reconstruction of a named target. The default full-disk architecture is broadly inspired by systems such as IRAS 08544−4431.

Whole disk, observing techniques & accretion

Whole disk shows a continuous, uncut, neutrally coloured disk using the same rim, height, flaring and outer radius as Probe layers. The whole-disk palette uses a muted copper-red inner rim grading into dark reddish-brown outer dust, distinct from the warm-white primary, with a darker underside and approximate illumination shading. The outer edge fades smoothly over the outermost 18% of the displayed radial span, rather than ending in a hard vertical wall. Nested shells with continuous opacity up to the surface fill the whole-disk body so the edge-on view does not collapse into transparent surfaces. This is approximate volume rendering for presentation, not radiative transfer. This visual taper does not alter the prescribed density or SED. Whole-disk opacity adjusts visual solidity independently of tracer strength and SED optical depth. These colours are illustrative material shading, not a temperature measurement or true-colour image. Probe layers adds approximate tracer sensitivity regions, which overlap and are not hard observational boundaries. Focus isolates a technique. Mid-IR does not automatically penetrate to the mid-plane; mm continuum can also be optically thick.

Accretion has a separate presentation preset. The circumbinary disk is a plausible gas reservoir feeding both the primary and the companion disk; jet studies favour circumbinary supply to the companion (Bollen et al. 2022), while photospheric depletion supports dust-poor reaccretion onto the primary. The ribbons indicate this connection without claiming recovered stream paths. Companion accretion disk independently hides its mesh and label in both the main view and inset; it does not physically delete a jet-launching structure or change gas feeding.

Improved rendering & shared approximation

The default selects one tracer on a neutral, translucent context disk. Smooth annular bands replace radial mesh wedges. Transparency is a display aid, not a calculated optical depth; there is no opaque disk mode. Depth-sorted bands and tapered shells provide approximate spatial ordering and a filled whole-disk silhouette, not a radiative-transfer image; star markers are drawn in front only in the explanatory layer views; Whole disk applies obscuration. The binary inset is independently enlarged and always explanatory. Density remains axisymmetric; phase-function shading and rim attenuation can produce brightness asymmetry without density asymmetry.

Geometry & motion

A luminous evolved primary and a smaller main-sequence companion sit inside a circumbinary dust cavity. The much fainter companion is marked without a stellar glow and has a compact accretion disk and bipolar outflow. The large dusty disk is flared, with a locally puffed, rounded inner rim. The rim puffing control scales its height above the underlying flared profile; this geometric adjustment is not a self-consistent irradiation or hydrostatic calculation. Approximate attenuation of central illumination is computed along rays to the adopted surface; a prescribed diffuse-heating floor prevents shadowed dust from being assigned zero temperature. This is not a radiative-equilibrium solution. A more extended gas component and broad disk outflow are shown separately from the companion jets. These components and their relative strengths differ between targets.

All scene lengths are dimensionless. Outer radii are compressed relative to the binary; stellar radii, the accretion disk and jet width are enlarged for legibility. There is no uniform AU scale. The orbital geometry is circular and coplanar, with an illustrative primary-to-secondary mass ratio of 0.6; real orbits may be eccentric or misaligned. The luminous star need not be the more massive star. Camera rotation changes only the viewpoint. Flow animation suggests rotation and outflow without calibrated speeds or times; it is not a dynamical simulation. The removed wedge and translucency are viewing aids, not missing material or measured optical depth.

Gas inside the dust rim & reaccretion

The optional pale-blue gas reservoir extends interior to the dust boundary. Warm-grey supply ribbons represent dust-poor gas reaching the post-AGB primary; blue-grey ribbons feed the circumcompanion accretion disk. This separates dust truncation/sublimation from gas extent. The innermost region is drawn as streams rather than a stable continuous circumbinary annulus through the stellar orbits. The inner gas need not be molecular or visible in the same CO transitions as the outer disk.

Bollen et al. (2020, 2022) favour the circumbinary disk as a source feeding the companion. Verhamme et al. (2024) and De Prins et al. (2024) model jets from the companion accretion disk. Kluska et al. (2022) link transition-disk structure with depletion; Mohorian et al. (2025) measure photospheric depletion that provides indirect constraints on dust–gas separation and reaccretion. These papers do not provide a unique spatial map of the streams displayed here.

Stream paths, gas inner radius, widths and speeds are illustrative. Supply ribbons follow prescribed curves that move with the binary, not hydrodynamical trajectories. A single arrow indicates direction and an optional moving highlight illustrates inward transport; there is no dense stream-particle display. Equal particle counts do not imply equal mass flow. No accretion ratio between the stars or jet ejection fraction is assumed. The inner gas switch controls both the reservoir and its streams; the two recipient switches independently hide their branches. The main-sequence companion designation is retained from the representative disk-bearing post-AGB jet literature; no specific brown-dwarf system is modelled.

Grain distribution, flaring and live SED

Grain mode uses three illustrative size bins drawn as smooth overlapping envelopes, with optional sparse example markers. The top of the small-grain population and the scattering highlight share the same geometry. Surface markers lie on this dust-bearing layer, not below an empty shell. Unmarked regions still contain continuous dust. The small-grain display switch hides both this population and its scattering highlight together. These switches do not remove matter from the SED opacity model. Small grains have the broadest vertical distribution; large grains settle toward the mid-plane and can be biased toward the inner dust boundary. Populations overlap. Each sampled ring is uniform in azimuth, with matched heights above and below the mid-plane. There are no prescribed dust spirals, clumps or azimuthal density asymmetries; the cutaway simply hides a viewing wedge. The optional marker radii are exaggerated and sample counts do not encode number or mass abundance. Scattering requires both grains and illumination; the surface represents an approximate stellar optical-depth layer, not a separate material boundary. This teaching geometry does not solve the optical-depth surface. It approximates rim shadowing using ray attenuation and polarized scattering with a Henyey–Greenstein intensity factor multiplied by a Rayleigh-like polarization factor. These factors are illustrative, not grain-specific Mie calculations. The assumed inner concentration is adjustable, not a universal measured property. Corporaal et al. (2023) find flaring and efficient settling in radiative-transfer models of IRAS 08544−4431.

The flaring exponent β changes the outer profile H ∝ rᵝ while keeping the unpuffed height at the dust rim fixed. β = 1 means constant aspect ratio, not a geometrically flat disk. The separate puffing control raises the rim locally.

The live SED integrates a shared two-population Gaussian dust-density profile along the line of sight from the primary to the observer and applies illustrative small-grain (λ⁻¹·³) and large-grain (λ⁻⁰·⁴⁵) opacity laws, weighted by their actual line-of-sight columns. Vertically integrated columns from the same Gaussian prescription set thermal emissivity, 1 − exp(−τλ). Dust emission integrates 120 contiguous logarithmic annuli with T(r) = 1500 K × (r/Rin)⁻⁰·⁶⁵ from Rin to 400 Rin, weighted by emitting area, then by 0.28 + 0.72 cos(i). This produces a single broad infrared excess rather than separate hot/cool dust peaks. The illustrative primary is a 6500 K blackbody. Only attenuated direct primary light and thermal dust emission are included in the SED; there is no scattered-light term or companion component. This deliberate two-component approximation does not claim scattering is negligible in every post-AGB system, particularly when direct starlight is strongly obscured. Its small, muted scene marker is for locating the companion, not a calibrated flux ratio. Each viewing angle is renormalized to a total peak of one, using the same factor for all components. The vertical axis is logarithmic; curves below 10⁻⁵ are omitted, not assigned zero flux. Normalization compares spectral shape, not absolute luminosity. Strong obscuration can suppress the stellar peak; two visible maxima are not artificially enforced at every angle. The spectral radius ratio of 400 is mapped logarithmically onto the displayed dust interval. The prescribed temperature profile now responds to relative stellar heating, cavity size and attenuated surface illumination; emissivity uses the same approximate small-/large-grain opacity laws. This is a consistent illustrative parameterization, not a physical calibration of display lengths. Neither absolute flux nor self-consistent temperatures, mineralogy, detailed grain opacities, energy balance or companion/accretion-disk emission is solved. Grain visibility switches affect only the drawing. Settling and inner concentration now alter the shared two-population density used for obscuration and surface illumination; the opacity laws remain prescribed power laws. The geometry is compressed, so occultation angles are explanatory rather than predictions for a real object.

Cutaway, zoom and tracer visibility never change the obscuring dust. In Probe layers, both stars are drawn over the disk as orientation markers. In Whole disk, each star is attenuated by its own line-of-sight dust column and participates in depth ordering, so the disk can hide it edge-on. The optional binary inset remains an unattenuated orientation diagram. The SED still includes physical attenuation in the approximate model. The polarimetric overlay uses a brightened display transfer function for readability; its brightness is not calibrated polarized intensity. Inclination sweep varies the viewing angle for demonstration, not physical precession; azimuth-only rotation need not strongly change the SED of an axisymmetric disk. Binary displacement can create small phase-dependent differences.

What the techniques actually probe

TechniqueInterpretation and limits
Polarimetric imagingOptical/near-IR starlight scattered by grains in illuminated disk surface layers. Polarized brightness depends on scattering angle, grain properties, illumination and resolution; it is not a direct density map. The resolved bright ring need not be the dust sublimation rim.
Near-IR interferometryHot inner dust and central components, with possible scattered and over-resolved flux. The inner dust rim can lie near sublimation in full disks or be farther out in transition disks. It does not isolate dust alone.
Mid-IR interferometryWarm dust across wavelength-dependent radii and emitting surfaces. It can constrain radial thermal structure, cavity size and mineralogy. An optically thick disk can hide its mid-plane; the orange volume is not a claim of direct mid-plane access.
Millimetre continuumCool dust, with sensitivity to larger grains and potentially deeper layers. Settling toward the mid-plane is a useful schematic, not a demonstrated universal geometry. Optical depth, temperature and opacity complicate grain-size and dust-mass inferences; continuum may be optically thick.
Molecular-line interferometryALMA/NOEMA-type CO maps constrain rotating gas and extended outflows. Emission depends on line excitation, abundance and opacity; CO can trace disk surfaces rather than all gas. The broad molecular outflow is distinct from the compact companion jet.
SEDIntegrated light from the unresolved system, with stellar, scattered and reprocessed dust contributions. It constrains the energy budget and dust temperatures but does not locate each emitting component uniquely. The optional live curve includes toy wavelength-dependent extinction along the primary-to-observer ray, projected thermal dust emission, with scattering intentionally omitted; it is not data or a fit.
Abundance spectroscopyPrimarily the post-AGB photosphere. Depletion patterns can support dust–gas separation followed by reaccretion of dust-poor gas. Separate circumstellar lines constrain surrounding material; photospheric abundances do not directly map the disk chemistry.
Jet spectroscopyPhase-dependent absorption/emission, including Hα, constrains a jet launched by the circumcompanion accretion disk. Detailed shape, inclination, rotation and launch physics remain model-dependent.
